## Overview

ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sales) and ROAS (Return on Advertising Spend) are two ways of expressing the same underlying ad-campaign performance: ACoS divides total ad spend by attributed ad sales to show the percentage of sales spent on advertising, while ROAS divides revenue generated by ad spend to show anticipated earnings per dollar spent, and it differs from ROI in that it measures a specific campaign's spend against the revenue it directly generated in the same period rather than total costs across a broader scope like agency fees. Break-even ACoS — profit divided by revenue (sales price minus total product cost and Amazon's fees) — represents the maximum percentage of the sales price that can be spent on PPC before the seller starts losing money, and a ROAS of roughly 2:1 is considered slightly above industry average, with 3:1 or 4:1 seen as a stronger target. Beyond ACoS/ROAS, Amazon also tracks new-to-brand metrics — first-time order counts and sales from customers new to a brand within a 12-month look-back window — and recommends using them alongside ACoS or ROAS, since brand-growth campaigns (such as those often run via Sponsored Brands) that drive first-time purchases can cost more than repeat-purchase campaigns, a nuance ACoS or ROAS alone would not capture.

## Facts

- ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sales) represents how many dollars of revenue resulted from each dollar spent on an ad campaign; it is calculated by dividing total ad spend by total attributed ad sales and expressed as a percentage. — [source](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-sponsored-product-ads/), confidence: Medium, last-checked: 2026-09-02
- Break-even ACoS can be calculated by dividing profit by revenue, where revenue is the sales price minus total product cost and Amazon's fees; this represents the maximum percentage of the sales price that can be spent on PPC before losing money. — [source](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-sponsored-product-ads/), confidence: Medium, last-checked: 2026-09-02
- New-to-brand metrics measure orders and sales generated from first-time customers of a brand on Amazon, tracking first-time order counts, the percentage of total orders that are new-to-brand, total new-to-brand sales, and the percentage of total sales that are new-to-brand, within a 12-month look-back window. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-brands), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-02
- Amazon recommends using new-to-brand metrics to supplement ACOS or ROAS, because driving first-time purchases (which Sponsored Brands often supports) can cost more than driving repeat purchases, so ACOS or ROAS alone would not capture the impact of a brand-growth campaign. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-brands), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-02
- Return on Advertising Spend (ROAS) is a marketing metric that measures the effectiveness of a specific ad campaign by comparing the revenue it generated to the amount spent on advertising. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/return-on-ad-spend-roas), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-07
- The ROAS formula is calculated by dividing the revenue generated from an ad campaign by the amount spent on that campaign. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/return-on-ad-spend-roas), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-07
- Unlike ROI, which accounts for total advertising spend across a broader scope (such as agency fees), ROAS measures a specific ad campaign against the revenue it directly generated during the same period. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/return-on-ad-spend-roas), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-07
- ROAS and Advertising Cost of Sales (ACOS) measure the same underlying performance but present it in different formats: ROAS indicates anticipated earnings from an ad campaign, while ACOS indicates the percentage of ad spend relative to sales. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/return-on-ad-spend-roas), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-07
- A ROAS ratio of 2:1 (earning $2 for every $1 of ad spend) is cited as slightly above the current industry average, while a ROAS closer to 3 or 4 is considered a stronger target. — [source](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/return-on-ad-spend-roas), confidence: High, last-checked: 2026-09-07
